## The rule

Most A/B tests I’ve seen end the same way: two weeks of traffic, a lift that looks real, and a p-value that won’t drop below 0.05. The usual cause isn’t bad luck. The test was too small to detect the effect it was looking for.

There’s a quick check you can run before you start. For a two-group test at 80% power and $\alpha = 0.05$, the sample size per group is roughly:

<Eq n={1}>

$$
n \approx \frac{16\sigma^2}{\delta^2}
$$

</Eq>

Here $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of your metric and $\delta$ is the smallest difference you care about.[^lehr] The $\delta^2$ in the denominator is the part that hurts. Halve the effect you want to detect and you need four times the users.

<Callout label="Worked example">
At a 10% conversion rate, $\sigma^2 \approx 0.09$. Detecting a lift to 11% takes about 14,400 users per group. Detecting a lift to 10.5% takes about 57,600.
</Callout>

## What to do instead

If the number is out of reach, you have three honest options. Test a bigger change, which raises $\delta$. Pick a less noisy metric, which lowers $\sigma$. Or wait until you have the traffic. Running the test anyway and hoping is not one of them.

[^lehr]: Lehr’s rule. The 16 comes from $2(z_{0.975} + z_{0.80})^2 = 2(1.96 + 0.84)^2 \approx 15.7$.
